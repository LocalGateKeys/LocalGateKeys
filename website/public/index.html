How to get started

You decide where encrypted copies go

Every device keeps its own encrypted vault. Nothing leaves a device unless you set it up to. Sync between your PC and iPhone is part of Pro.

  • Nowhere

    The default, in every edition

    The vault stays on your device. Scheduled backups are encrypted files you keep where you like, including a USB drive or a sync folder.

  • Your home Wi-Fi

    Pro

    Pair your PC and iPhone once with a one-use QR code. When they are on the same network and unlocked, they sync directly over an encrypted connection. No internet connection is involved.

  • Your own cloud folder

    Pro, if you turn it on

    Point sync at a cloud folder you already use, such as Dropbox or iCloud Drive, protected by a sync password. Your cloud provider stores encrypted files it cannot read. We are not involved at all.

What it does for you every day

  • Fills sign-ins in Chrome and Edge

    The browser extension talks to the LocalGateKeys app on your PC through a small local helper. It only offers logins saved for the exact site you are on. It also fills two-factor codes, addresses and payment cards, and offers to save new logins after you sign in.

    Passkeys work too: a website can save a passkey in your vault and sign you in with it, and you approve each one in the desktop app.

    The extension never receives your whole vault, and no request leaves your computer.

  • And in Windows apps

    Desktop app fill works with sign-in windows of Windows programs such as game launchers. You bind a window once; after that a fill button beside the password field, or a hotkey, fills the saved fields.

    It reads only the accessibility information Windows exposes about a window. It never records your keystrokes, never injects code into another program, and refuses to fill if the app or window has changed.

  • Two-factor codes built in Pro

    Save a site's two-factor setup key with its login, and the current code appears at the top of the login and on the One-time codes card on Home. Scan the site's QR code from a screenshot or your webcam, or bring codes over from Google Authenticator. On iPhone, you can show the codes you choose on your Apple Watch.

    Codes are worked out on your own devices. Two-factor codes are part of Pro.

  • Checks your passwords

    The generator makes strong passwords or passphrases. Password health shows which saved passwords are weak, reused, old or missing a two-factor code, and the iPhone app gives the same check a score. If you turn it on, a breach check you start compares them with known breaches, sending only five characters of a hash.

  • On your iPhone too

    The iPhone app fills logins in Safari and other apps, and cards and identities in any text field on iOS 18 and later. It has a security check, password history, expiry reminders and Recently deleted with Undo, and it opens with Face ID, Touch ID or an optional app password.

    It syncs with your PC, and buying Pro on the iPhone unlocks Windows too.

  • Share one item, safely

    Send a login or note as a file or a QR code, locked with a six-word code that you tell the other person another way, such as by phone.

    It opens once, and three wrong codes destroy it.
