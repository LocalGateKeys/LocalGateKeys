Everything it does, and where it does it

All of this runs on your own devices: the Windows app, the browser extension and the iPhone app. Free holds up to 7 items; Pro removes the limit and adds sync, two-factor codes and documents.

The vault

Encrypted on your disk
Everything you save is encrypted with a key protected by your master password, using Argon2id and AES-256-GCM. Names, usernames and websites are encrypted too, not just passwords.
More than passwords
Website and desktop app logins, passkeys, payment cards, bank accounts, identities, secure notes, documents, SSH keys, API keys, Wi-Fi networks, bookmarks, software licences, subscriptions, warranties and purchases, each with its own fields.
Documents (Pro)
Keep files such as scanned documents in the vault, up to 25 MB each. Each file is encrypted with its own key. Preview a document, save a copy or open it in another app. With sync, files travel to your other devices, still encrypted.
Two-factor codes (Pro)
Store a site's setup key and LocalGateKeys shows the current code at the top of the login, and on a One-time codes card on the Home page. Codes follow the TOTP standard (RFC 6238): 6 to 8 digits, 15 to 120 seconds, SHA-1, SHA-256 or SHA-512.
Codes on Apple Watch (Pro)
Show the two-factor codes you choose on your Apple Watch, up to 10. The watch shows codes only, never passwords, and they keep working without the iPhone nearby.
Scan a QR code
Choose Scan QR code and paste a snip of the site's two-factor QR code (Win+Shift+S), drop in an image, or hold it up to your webcam. Google Authenticator export QR codes work too. The image is read on your PC and never saved.
Password generator
Random passwords of 8 to 256 characters, or passphrases of 3 to 16 words.
Password health
See which saved passwords are weak, reused, more than a year old or missing a two-factor code. Breach checks against Have I Been Pwned are off until you turn them on, run only when you start them, and send only five characters of a hash, never the password.
Site logos and kind icons
Each item shows the site's icon, a built-in logo for well-known sites, or an icon for its kind, so you can spot it at a glance.
Search, sort and collections
Find items with a command palette (Ctrl+K), filter chips, favourites and your own collections. Sort the list by title, date modified or date created, with favourites first. Arrow keys, Enter and Ctrl+C work in the list straight from the search box.
Trash and undo
Deleted items go to the trash, and the message that confirms it has an Undo button. The trash empties itself after 30 days, or keeps everything until you empty it.
Password history
When a login's password changes, up to 10 earlier passwords are kept for a year in its encrypted history. Copy one, or restore it.
Verified site
The browser extension marks a saved site as a Verified site and warns before you sign in or save on a lookalike address, such as a swapped letter. The check runs on your device and your saved sites never leave the app. Safari keeps a sealed list of site names only. On Android, the autofill save prompt shows the same verdict and asks before saving on a lookalike address.

Moving in and out

Import from other password managers
Bring in exports from 1Password, Bitwarden, LastPass, Dashlane, Proton Pass, KeePass and KeePassXC (including .kdbx files), Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox and Safari, or any other CSV file. You review every item before anything is written, and the import never goes online. On iPhone, bring logins, two-factor codes and passkeys straight over from Apple Passwords (iOS 26 and later), or import a file from many password managers and authenticator apps.
Export
Export to an encrypted KeePass KDBX 4 file, or to Bitwarden-compatible CSV or JSON, or KeePass XML. Every export asks for your master password first.
Share one item securely
Send a login or note as a .lgkshare file or a QR code, locked with a six-word code that you tell the other person another way. It opens once, and three wrong codes destroy it. Passkeys, documents and items with an attached file cannot be shared.

Filling sign-ins

Chrome and Edge extension
Fill and save logins on websites, including sign-ins that ask for the username and password on separate pages. The extension asks the app on your PC for logins that match the exact site, through a local helper. It also fills two-factor codes, addresses and payment cards.
Save prompts you control
After you sign in, the extension offers to save a new login or update a changed password. You can turn the prompt off for any site from the toolbar button.
You start every fill
Nothing is filled until you pick an account. Forms are not submitted for you unless you turn on "Sign in after filling" in Settings, and then only after a fill you started.
Passkeys
When a website in Chrome or Edge offers a passkey, LocalGateKeys can save it in your vault and sign you in with it. You approve every passkey in the desktop app, with Windows Hello or your master password, and the browser's own passkey dialog is always one click away. The private key never leaves the app.
Windows app fill
Fill sign-in windows of Windows programs with a fill button beside the password field or a hotkey (Ctrl+Shift+Space). Each app is bound once, and fill is refused if the program, its window or its fields have changed since.
Quick access
Search your vault and copy from anywhere with a hotkey (Ctrl+Alt+K by default). Ctrl+Alt+L locks the vault at once.
No keylogging, ever
LocalGateKeys installs no keyboard hook, reads no screen content to find passwords, and injects nothing into other programs. If an app does not expose proper sign-in fields, it is not filled.

Unlocking

Master password
At least 12 characters, never written to disk. It is the one thing that opens your vault, and we cannot reset it.
Windows Hello
Unlock with your fingerprint, face or PIN through Windows Hello, after you set it up in LocalGateKeys.
Camera check
An optional webcam face check for PCs without Windows Hello. It is a convenience, not certified anti-spoofing; prefer Windows Hello when you can.
Recovery phrase
An optional 24-word phrase that can open the vault if you forget the master password. It is shown once and never stored by the app.
Auto-lock and clipboard clearing
The vault locks after a period you choose, and copied passwords are cleared from the clipboard. Only what LocalGateKeys copied is cleared.

Backups

Scheduled encrypted backups
Daily or weekly, at a time you set. Each backup is a full encrypted file, checked after writing, with attachments included.
Extra copy in a folder you choose
Have each backup copied to a OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox or Proton Drive folder, or anywhere else. Only encrypted backup files are copied, never the live vault.
Encrypted transfer packages
Move selected items to another vault in an encrypted file protected by its own password.
Phone backups
The iPhone and Android apps save an encrypted .lgkm backup with a password you choose. Restore it on any iPhone, Android phone, Mac or PC: it merges like sync and never wipes your vault.

Emergency access and duress password (Pro)

Emergency access
Split your recovery phrase into up to five shards for people you trust. Any chosen number of them (two to five) can get back in. Each person gets a signed file, a QR code or a printed sheet. There is no account and no server: you share a folder, such as a cloud folder or a USB stick, with them.
Waiting period and veto
A person who needs access asks from their own LocalGateKeys. After the waiting period you chose (1 to 30 days) the app opens a copy of your latest encrypted backup. Until then, any of your devices can say No. You can also grant access at once. Phones end the flow by showing your recovery phrase; open the backup on a computer.
The limit
Nothing outside the apps enforces the waiting period or your No. Any group of holders that holds enough shards, working together, can open your backup at once. Choose people who would not act together and keep the shards apart. Replacing your recovery phrase makes old shards useless.
Duress password
A second password opens a believable vault with sample items and shows nothing about your real one. While it is open, the real vault stays locked for the hours you choose, and nothing syncs. It hides your vault from someone looking over your shoulder or forcing you to open the app. It does not hide that the feature exists from someone who examines your files. On Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android.

Power-user tools

Backup restore test
Once a week, LocalGateKeys restores your newest backup into a scratch folder, checks that it opens, and deletes the copy. It tells you if the backup uses an old password.
Password change assistant
Opens the page where you change a site's password: from a curated list, the standard /.well-known/change-password address, or the sign-in page. It never fills in third-party sites.
Several vaults (Pro)
Keep separate vaults, such as Work and Personal. The free plan holds one vault. The 7-item free limit counts across all vaults.
Printable vault health report
A one-page report you can print. It contains no passwords and no item names.
Expiring and revocable shares
Shares can expire after 1 hour or 1 day, work only once, and be revoked. This is best effort.
Estate pack template
A template for executors, next to emergency access.

For developers and AI agents

AI agents sign in, your passwords stay on your PC (Pro)
Claude Code and other MCP agents can ask LocalGateKeys to sign in to a site. You approve each request, the browser extension fills the form, and the agent never sees the password. On Windows: claude mcp add localgatekeys -- LocalGateKeysNativeHost.exe mcp (the Microsoft Store app also runs the lgk command line as LocalGateKeysNativeHost.exe lgk). AI agents can sign in, your passwords stay on your PC
Tamper-evident access log
Each activity entry is linked to the one before it by a hash, so the Security check shows when the log was changed, cut short or emptied.

Sync between your devices (Pro)

Over your home Wi-Fi
Pair a PC or an iPhone once with a one-use QR code (between PCs you paste its text). When both are unlocked on the same network, they exchange changes directly over an authenticated, encrypted connection. Nothing goes through the internet. If a PC's address changes, Find again reconnects, and each device shows when it last synced.
Through your own cloud folder
If your devices are rarely on the same network, sync through a cloud folder you already use, such as Dropbox or iCloud Drive, protected by a sync password. The provider only ever stores encrypted files. You can use both routes.
What syncs
Logins, notes, cards and your other items, and attached documents with their files. Collections stay on each device. Edits made on two devices at once keep both versions, and a delete moves the item to the trash on the others. If a device brings back an older copy of the vault, the Windows app warns you instead of accepting it.
iPhone
The iPhone app syncs with your Windows PC and Mac. A free PC paired with an iPhone that has Pro takes the licence over sync and becomes Pro. Get it on the App Store.

On iPhone

AutoFill in Safari and apps
Fill logins in Safari and other apps, with suggestions in the QuickType bar, and use LocalGateKeys for passkeys. On iOS 18 and later it also fills cards, addresses and other details in any text field. The Safari extension offers to save a new login after you sign in.
Face ID, Touch ID or an app password
The vault opens with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode, with no password to create. If others know your passcode, add an optional app password, backed by a 24-word recovery phrase.
Security check
The Security tab gives your vault a score and lists weak, reused and old passwords, and logins that could use two-factor codes. Breach checks stay off until you turn them on.
Recently deleted, history and reminders
Deleted items wait 30 days in Recently deleted, and Undo brings one straight back. Every login keeps its password history, and you can get a reminder before a card, passport or licence expires.
Attached files (Pro)
Attach documents and photos to your vault, stored encrypted and synced with your other devices.
Ten languages
The iPhone app speaks English, German, Spanish, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Dutch, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese and Simplified Chinese.

Privacy

No account
You never sign up or sign in. A Pro licence is a key you keep, checked on your device. On iPhone, Pro is an App Store purchase, and the app sends only the App Store receipt to our licence server.
No vault telemetry
The apps send no usage data, crash reports or analytics.
Strict offline mode
On by default. It blocks background internet requests and breach checks, and an update check asks you first.
Website icons, your choice
To show a site's icon, the app downloads it from that site. This tells the site you have an account there, and nothing else. It is off by default: turn on "Show website icons" in Settings (strict offline mode must be off).
Update checks you start
Checking for updates downloads one small signed file. The app never downloads or installs anything by itself.
